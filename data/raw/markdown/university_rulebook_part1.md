1. General Provisions
1.1 Purpose

This rulebook establishes the academic and administrative policies applicable to students enrolled at Meridian University. It defines eligibility requirements, student responsibilities, academic procedures, administrative processes, and the authority of university committees.

Students are responsible for familiarizing themselves with the policies applicable to their programme, semester, and academic activity. Ignorance of a published rule does not normally exempt a student from compliance.

1.2 Scope

These regulations apply to undergraduate and postgraduate students unless a programme-specific regulation explicitly states otherwise.

Where a programme-specific regulation exists, it must be read together with this rulebook. A programme-specific regulation does not automatically override a university-wide rule unless the document expressly grants such an exception.

1.3 Definitions

For the purpose of this rulebook:

Academic term means the period beginning on the official commencement date of classes and ending on the final date specified in the academic calendar.
Attendance means participation recorded by the university for scheduled academic activities.
Semester examination means an examination conducted at the end of an academic term for a registered course.
Approved leave means leave formally authorized through the applicable university process.
Academic authority means an officer, department, board, or committee authorized to make an academic decision under these regulations.
2. Attendance Policy
2.1 Attendance Requirement

Students are expected to attend all scheduled lectures, tutorials, laboratory sessions, seminars, and other compulsory academic activities.

A student must maintain a minimum attendance of 75% in each registered course to remain eligible for the regular semester examination in that course.

Attendance is calculated using the total number of scheduled sessions for the course during the applicable semester.

2.2 Attendance Records

Each academic department must maintain attendance records using the university-approved attendance system.

Students should review their attendance records periodically. If a student believes that an attendance entry is incorrect, the student must report the issue to the course instructor within seven calendar days of the relevant class or publication of the attendance record.

Requests submitted after this period may be considered only when the student provides a reasonable explanation and supporting evidence.

2.3 Short Attendance

A student whose attendance falls below the minimum requirement may be declared ineligible for the regular semester examination.

The department may issue an attendance warning when a student's attendance falls below 80%, allowing the student an opportunity to improve attendance before the examination eligibility list is finalized.

An attendance warning does not itself constitute a declaration of examination ineligibility.

2.4 Attendance Calculation

Attendance percentage is calculated as:

Attendance Percentage = (Eligible Sessions Attended / Eligible Sessions Conducted) × 100

Sessions officially cancelled by the university are not included in the denominator.

Where an academic activity is officially rescheduled, attendance is recorded against the rescheduled activity.

2.5 Laboratory Attendance

Laboratory and practical sessions are treated as compulsory academic activities where the programme or course requires them.

A department may prescribe additional laboratory attendance requirements when those requirements are published in the course information before the beginning of the semester.

3. Examination Eligibility
3.1 General Eligibility

A student may appear for a regular semester examination only when all applicable academic and administrative eligibility requirements have been satisfied.

Eligibility normally includes:

meeting the minimum attendance requirement;
completing required internal assessments;
completing mandatory laboratory or practical work where applicable;
completing required examination registration;
satisfying applicable fee requirements; and
having no active disciplinary restriction that prevents examination participation.
3.2 Attendance-Based Eligibility

Unless an approved exception applies, students must maintain 75% attendance in a course to be eligible for its semester examination.

The final eligibility list is prepared by the relevant academic department and verified by the examination office.

Students who do not meet the attendance requirement should not assume that submission of an examination form creates examination eligibility.

3.3 Eligibility List

The examination office may publish a provisional eligibility list before the examination period.

Students should review the list and report apparent errors within the period specified in the examination notice.

A correction request must include sufficient information for the university to verify the student's claim.

3.4 Examination Registration

Eligible students must complete examination registration within the announced registration period.

Failure to complete registration may result in the student being excluded from the examination schedule even when academic eligibility requirements have otherwise been satisfied.

Late registration may be permitted only when the examination office explicitly announces a late-registration facility.

4. Internal Assessment
4.1 Components

Internal assessment may include quizzes, assignments, laboratory work, presentations, projects, class tests, mid-semester examinations, or other academic activities specified for the course.

The course instructor must communicate the assessment components and their relative weightings to students through the approved academic communication channel.

4.2 Submission Requirements

Students must submit assessed work by the deadlines announced by the instructor or department.

A late submission may receive a reduced mark or may be rejected where the applicable course policy permits such treatment.

4.3 Missing Assessment

A student who misses a compulsory assessment should inform the course instructor or department as soon as reasonably possible.

Where the absence is supported by acceptable documentation, the department may provide an alternative assessment opportunity.

An alternative assessment is not guaranteed merely because a student requests one.

4.4 Academic Integrity

Students must complete assessed work honestly.

Submitting another person's work as one's own, unauthorized collaboration, fabrication of results, impersonation, or use of prohibited assistance may constitute academic misconduct.

Academic misconduct may result in penalties under the disciplinary regulations.

5. Examination Rules
5.1 Examination Conduct

Students must arrive at the examination venue at least 20 minutes before the scheduled starting time unless the examination notice specifies a different reporting requirement.

Students must carry the identification or authorization document required by the examination office.

5.2 Permitted Materials

Only materials explicitly permitted in the examination instructions may be brought into the examination room.

Electronic devices, including mobile phones and smart watches, are prohibited unless the examination instructions expressly permit their use.

5.3 Late Arrival

A student arriving after the scheduled start time may be admitted at the discretion of the examination authority.

A student arriving more than 30 minutes after the scheduled start time will normally not be permitted to enter the examination room.

5.4 Leaving the Examination Room

Students may not normally leave the examination room during the first 30 minutes of an examination.

Permission to leave temporarily may be granted by the invigilator for exceptional circumstances.

5.5 Examination Misconduct

Unauthorized communication, possession of prohibited materials, copying, impersonation, or any attempt to obtain or provide unauthorized assistance may be treated as examination misconduct.

Suspected misconduct must be documented and reported through the examination office's prescribed process.

6. Fee Payment
6.1 Tuition Fees

Students must pay tuition and other applicable academic fees by the deadlines published in the official fee schedule.

The university may apply a late-payment charge when payment is received after the published deadline.

6.2 Examination Fees

Where an examination fee applies, the fee must be paid before the examination registration deadline.

Payment of an examination fee does not by itself establish examination eligibility.

6.3 Outstanding Dues

Students with outstanding university dues may be subject to administrative restrictions as permitted by university regulations.

A restriction must be communicated through an official university channel.

6.4 Fee Disputes

A student who believes that a fee has been incorrectly assessed should submit a fee query to the designated administrative office.

The query should include the student's university identification number, the relevant fee item, and supporting documentation where applicable.

7. Student Records
7.1 Accuracy of Records

Students are responsible for ensuring that their name, programme, contact information, and other required student-record information are accurate.

Changes to official records must be requested through the applicable university process.

7.2 Academic Documents

Official transcripts, certificates, grade reports, and other academic documents may be issued only through authorized university procedures.

Students should not alter or reproduce official academic documents in a manner that could misrepresent their authenticity.

7.3 Privacy of Records

Student records must be handled in accordance with applicable university privacy and records-management requirements.

Access to restricted academic information may be limited to authorized persons or offices.

8. Academic Calendar
8.1 Official Calendar

The university publishes an academic calendar specifying important academic dates, including the commencement of classes, assessment periods, examination periods, holidays, and registration deadlines.

Students are responsible for checking the current academic calendar and official notices.

8.2 Changes to Dates

The university may modify academic dates when necessary because of operational, academic, emergency, or regulatory circumstances.

Changes to published dates should be communicated through an official university channel.

8.3 Examination Schedule

The examination office publishes the examination timetable before the examination period.

Students are responsible for checking the timetable and reporting apparent scheduling conflicts through the prescribed process.

9. General Student Responsibilities

Students must comply with university regulations, official instructions, and reasonable directions issued by authorized university personnel.

Students are expected to maintain professional conduct in academic and administrative settings.

A student's failure to comply with a university requirement may result in an administrative or academic consequence where such consequence is authorized by the applicable regulation.

Where a student believes that a decision has been made incorrectly, the student may use the applicable review or appeal process rather than disregarding the decision.
