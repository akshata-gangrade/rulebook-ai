10. Leave Policy
10.1 General Leave

Students may request leave from academic activities when circumstances prevent them from attending scheduled classes.

Leave does not automatically remove the academic consequences of an absence. Students remain responsible for completing academic work missed during approved leave.

10.2 Leave Categories

The university recognizes the following broad categories of leave:

medical leave;
family or emergency leave;
university-authorized activity leave; and
other exceptional leave approved by the competent authority.

Each category may have different documentation and approval requirements.

10.3 Leave Application

A student requesting leave should submit the applicable leave request through the university's designated process.

The request should normally identify:

the reason for leave;
the dates of absence;
the courses or academic activities affected; and
supporting documentation where required.

Submitting a leave request does not mean that the request has been approved.

10.4 Approved Leave

Leave is considered approved only after the competent university authority accepts the request.

A student should not assume that an application is approved merely because it has been submitted.

Approved leave must be recorded through the university's designated student or academic records system.

10.5 Academic Responsibilities During Leave

Students taking approved leave remain responsible for academic requirements that can reasonably be completed during or after the leave period.

An instructor may provide reasonable arrangements for missed academic activities when permitted by the applicable course policy.

11. Medical Exemptions
11.1 Medical Circumstances

A student who is unable to attend academic activities because of a medical condition may request consideration under the university's medical exemption process.

The request must be supported by appropriate medical documentation where documentation is required.

11.2 Medical Documentation

Medical documentation should normally identify the period during which the student was medically unable to participate in academic activities.

The university may request clarification when the submitted documentation is incomplete, unclear, or inconsistent with the dates claimed by the student.

11.3 Review of Medical Requests

Medical exemption requests are reviewed by the authority designated by the university.

The reviewing authority may approve, partially approve, or reject a request based on the available documentation and applicable regulations.

11.4 Attendance Consideration

Approved medical circumstances may be considered when evaluating a student's attendance record.

However, approval of a medical request does not automatically guarantee eligibility for a semester examination.

The student's examination eligibility must be determined according to the applicable examination and attendance regulations.

11.5 Extended Medical Absence

Where a student's medical absence extends for a substantial portion of the academic term, the university may recommend academic adjustments, temporary withdrawal, deferred assessment, or another appropriate academic arrangement.

Any such arrangement requires approval through the applicable university process.

12. Emergency and Family Leave
12.1 Emergency Circumstances

Students may request emergency leave for circumstances that require their immediate absence from the university.

Examples may include serious family emergencies, unexpected events, or other circumstances recognized by the competent authority.

12.2 Supporting Evidence

Where appropriate, students may be asked to provide supporting evidence.

The university should consider the nature of the emergency when determining what evidence is reasonable.

12.3 Approval

Emergency leave is not considered approved until the competent authority has accepted the request.

In urgent circumstances, preliminary notification may be provided before complete documentation is available.

The student may subsequently be required to submit supporting documentation.

13. University-Authorized Activities
13.1 Official Representation

Students representing the university in an officially authorized academic, cultural, sporting, technical, or other activity may request attendance consideration for the period of the authorized activity.

13.2 Authorization

The activity must be approved by the relevant university authority before it can be treated as an officially authorized university activity.

Participation in an independently organized event does not automatically qualify as university-authorized activity.

13.3 Academic Arrangements

Where an officially authorized activity causes a student to miss an assessment or compulsory academic activity, the relevant department may provide an alternative arrangement where permitted by academic regulations.

The student remains responsible for completing applicable academic requirements.

14. Scholarships
14.1 Scholarship Eligibility

University scholarships may be awarded based on academic performance, financial circumstances, achievement, participation, or other criteria specified in the applicable scholarship notice.

Each scholarship may have its own eligibility and continuation requirements.

14.2 Academic Performance

Where academic performance is a scholarship requirement, the applicable scholarship notice must specify the required performance threshold.

A student who fails to satisfy a continuation requirement may become ineligible for subsequent scholarship installments.

14.3 Attendance Requirements

A scholarship may include an attendance requirement when the applicable scholarship terms expressly specify one.

Where no scholarship-specific attendance requirement is published, general university attendance regulations continue to apply.

14.4 Scholarship Review

Students may request a review of a scholarship decision when the applicable scholarship procedure permits review.

The request should identify the decision being challenged and provide relevant supporting information.

15. Student Support
15.1 Academic Advising

Students may seek academic advice from their assigned academic advisor, programme coordinator, or another authorized academic support office.

Academic advising may cover course selection, academic progress, examination eligibility, attendance concerns, and other academic matters.

15.2 Attendance Concerns

A student who anticipates difficulty meeting attendance requirements should contact the relevant academic office as early as possible.

The university may provide guidance regarding available leave, medical, academic, or administrative procedures.

Students should not rely on informal assurances as a substitute for formal approval.

15.3 Support During Exceptional Circumstances

Students experiencing exceptional circumstances may contact the appropriate student support office.

The university may explain available procedures but cannot guarantee that a particular request will be approved.

16. Review and Appeals
16.1 Right to Review

Where university regulations provide a review or appeal mechanism, an affected student may submit a request within the applicable deadline.

16.2 Review Request

A review request should normally include:

the decision being challenged;
the reason for requesting review;
relevant supporting documents; and
any information that the student believes was not considered previously.
16.3 Attendance Decisions

A student who believes that an attendance-related decision was based on an incorrect record may request a review.

The university may verify attendance records, course schedules, approved leave records, and other relevant information before making a determination.

16.4 Examination Eligibility Appeals

Where permitted, a student declared ineligible for an examination may submit an appeal through the designated academic process.

Submission of an appeal does not automatically suspend the original eligibility decision.

16.5 Finality of Decisions

A decision made by the final designated reviewing authority under the applicable procedure is normally treated as final unless another review mechanism is expressly provided.

17. Student Conduct
17.1 General Conduct

Students are expected to behave respectfully toward other students, faculty members, staff, visitors, and university authorities.

Conduct that materially disrupts academic activities or university operations may be subject to disciplinary review.

17.2 False Information

Providing materially false information in an academic or administrative application may constitute misconduct.

This may include knowingly submitting fabricated documents or making false declarations to obtain an academic or administrative benefit.

17.3 University Property

Students must use university facilities and property responsibly.

Damage caused intentionally or through serious negligence may result in disciplinary or financial consequences under applicable regulations.

18. Administrative Communication
18.1 Official Notices

University decisions, deadlines, examination announcements, and policy changes should be communicated through official university channels.

Students are responsible for checking the communication channels designated for their programme.

18.2 Informal Communication

Information received from classmates, unofficial groups, social media, or other informal sources should not be treated as an official university decision unless subsequently confirmed through an official channel.

18.3 Responsibility to Check Notices

Failure to read an official notice does not normally invalidate the notice or extend the deadline stated in it.
