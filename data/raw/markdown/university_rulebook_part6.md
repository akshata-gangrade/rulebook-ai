48. Student Welfare
48.1 Student Welfare Services

The university provides student welfare services intended to support students in academic, administrative, residential, and other university-related matters.

Students may contact the designated student welfare office when they require guidance regarding available university procedures or support services.

48.2 Academic Advising

Academic advising may assist students with course planning, academic progress, attendance concerns, examination requirements, programme completion, and other academic matters.

Students remain responsible for making final decisions concerning their academic requirements unless a decision is formally made by an authorized university authority.

48.3 Student Support Appointments

Students may request appointments with designated support offices when an appointment system is available.

Students should provide sufficient information about the nature of their request so that it can be directed to the appropriate office.

48.4 Confidentiality

Information provided to student support personnel should be handled according to applicable university confidentiality requirements.

Disclosure may occur where required by university policy, law, safety obligations, or an authorized institutional process.

49. Accessibility and Academic Accommodation
49.1 Accessibility

The university seeks to provide reasonable access to academic and campus facilities for students who require accessibility support.

Students requiring an academic accommodation should contact the designated university office as early as reasonably possible.

49.2 Accommodation Requests

An accommodation request should identify the academic activity or university service for which support is being requested.

Where supporting documentation is required, students should submit the documentation through the designated process.

49.3 Academic Adjustments

Approved academic accommodations may include reasonable adjustments to examination arrangements, classroom access, assessment arrangements, or other academic processes where permitted.

An accommodation does not remove the essential academic requirements of a course or programme unless the applicable authority expressly approves such an adjustment.

49.4 Examination Accommodations

Students requiring examination accommodations should submit their request before the deadline specified by the examination office.

The examination office may coordinate with academic departments and accessibility personnel to implement approved arrangements.

49.5 Temporary Circumstances

Students experiencing temporary circumstances that affect participation in academic activities may seek guidance regarding available university procedures.

The appropriate authority determines whether an accommodation or other academic arrangement can be approved.

50. Student Organizations
50.1 Registration

Student organizations operating under the university's name must complete the registration process specified by the university.

Registration may require identification of office bearers, faculty advisors, objectives, and proposed activities.

50.2 Faculty Advisor

A registered student organization may be required to have a faculty or staff advisor.

The advisor may provide guidance concerning university procedures, safety requirements, and responsible use of university resources.

50.3 Events

Student organizations planning events on university premises must obtain required approvals before conducting the event.

The organization may be required to submit information concerning the proposed date, venue, expected attendance, security requirements, and other operational matters.

50.4 Financial Records

Student organizations receiving university funds must maintain appropriate records of income and expenditure.

The university may require financial reports or supporting documentation.

50.5 Responsible Conduct

Student organizations must ensure that their activities comply with applicable university regulations.

Registration of an organization does not exempt its members from individual responsibility for misconduct.

51. Campus Events
51.1 Event Approval

Events involving university facilities must be approved through the applicable administrative process.

Approval may be subject to availability of facilities, safety requirements, academic schedules, and other operational considerations.

51.2 Event Timing

Events should be scheduled so that they do not materially interfere with scheduled academic activities.

The university may require an event to be rescheduled where a significant academic or operational conflict exists.

51.3 Safety Requirements

Event organizers must comply with applicable safety requirements.

The university may require additional safety arrangements for events involving large numbers of participants or activities presenting increased risk.

51.4 External Participants

Events involving external guests or organizations may require additional approval.

Event organizers are responsible for following visitor and access requirements communicated by the university.

52. Research Activities
52.1 Student Research

Students conducting research under university supervision must comply with applicable academic and research requirements.

Research activities should be conducted responsibly and in accordance with approved procedures.

52.2 Research Supervision

Where supervision is required, students should work under the direction of an approved faculty supervisor or research authority.

The supervisor may review research plans, methodology, documentation, and progress.

52.3 Research Ethics

Research involving human participants, personal information, animals, hazardous materials, or other regulated areas may require prior approval from the appropriate university authority.

Students must obtain required approval before beginning regulated research activities.

52.4 Research Data

Research data should be collected, stored, and handled responsibly.

Students must follow applicable university requirements concerning data security, confidentiality, retention, and authorized access.

52.5 Research Integrity

Students must accurately represent research methods, observations, results, and sources.

Fabrication, falsification, plagiarism, or deliberate misrepresentation of research findings may constitute academic misconduct.

53. Intellectual Property
53.1 Student Work

Students generally retain rights in their original academic work subject to applicable university policies, funding conditions, contractual obligations, and intellectual-property requirements.

53.2 University Resources

Where a work is developed using substantial university resources or as part of an arrangement assigning intellectual-property rights, additional requirements may apply.

Students should seek clarification before commercially exploiting work created under such arrangements.

53.3 Disclosure of Research

Students should consider applicable confidentiality and intellectual-property requirements before publicly disclosing research results.

Where a disclosure could affect intellectual-property protection, students should seek guidance from the appropriate university office.

54. Grievances
54.1 General Grievance

A student who believes that a university process has been applied incorrectly or unfairly may submit a grievance through the applicable procedure.

A grievance should identify the decision, process, or event being challenged.

54.2 Informal Resolution

Where appropriate, students should first attempt to resolve routine concerns through the relevant instructor, programme coordinator, department, or administrative office.

Informal discussion does not prevent a student from using a formal procedure where such procedure is available.

54.3 Formal Grievance

A formal grievance should normally include:

a description of the issue;
relevant dates;
the persons or offices involved;
supporting documents; and
the resolution sought.
54.4 Review

The designated grievance authority may review relevant records, communications, policies, and other information before making a decision.

The authority may request additional information from the student or other persons where necessary.

54.5 Grievance Outcome

The outcome of a formal grievance should be communicated through an official university channel.

Where an appeal mechanism exists, the communication should identify the applicable appeal process.

55. Complaints Concerning University Services
55.1 Service Complaints

Students may submit complaints concerning university services when the applicable complaint mechanism is available.

Examples may include concerns about administrative processing, facilities, hostel services, library services, or other university services.

55.2 Complaint Submission

A complaint should contain sufficient information to allow the university to identify the service, event, or decision being challenged.

Anonymous complaints may be considered where permitted by the applicable procedure, although the university may be unable to investigate matters requiring additional information.

55.3 Investigation

The responsible office may investigate a complaint and consult other university offices when necessary.

The university may decline to investigate complaints that fall outside its jurisdiction or repeat matters already finally determined through an applicable process.

56. Records and Documentation
56.1 Official Records

University offices must maintain official records relevant to their assigned functions.

Records may include applications, decisions, attendance records, examination records, financial transactions, correspondence, and other administrative documents.

56.2 Record Retention

Records should be retained according to the university's applicable records-retention requirements.

Different categories of records may have different retention periods.

56.3 Student Copies

Students should retain copies of important applications, approvals, receipts, examination documents, and other records where appropriate.

56.4 Correction of Records

A student who identifies an apparent error in an official record may request correction through the relevant office.

The university may verify the underlying information before making a correction.

57. Deadlines and Extensions
57.1 Published Deadlines

Students must comply with deadlines stated in official university notices, academic calendars, application forms, or other authorized communications.

57.2 Request for Extension

Where a procedure permits an extension, the student should submit the request before the relevant deadline whenever reasonably possible.

The request should explain the reason for the extension and provide supporting evidence where appropriate.

57.3 Approval of Extension

An extension is valid only after it has been approved by the authority authorized to grant it.

Submitting an extension request does not automatically extend the deadline.

57.4 Exceptional Circumstances

An authority may consider exceptional circumstances when deciding whether an extension should be granted.

The existence of an exceptional circumstance does not automatically create a right to an extension.

58. Administrative Decision-Making
58.1 Authorized Decisions

University decisions must be made by the office, officer, or committee authorized under the applicable regulations.

58.2 Evidence

An authority may consider relevant records, applications, supporting documents, academic information, and other evidence when making a decision.

58.3 Reasons

Where required by the applicable procedure, the authority should record or communicate the basis for its decision.

58.4 Delegation

An authorized officer may delegate a function when university regulations permit delegation.

A delegated decision remains subject to the requirements applicable to the delegated function.

58.5 Record of Decision

Material administrative decisions should be recorded in the appropriate university system or official record.

59. Interpretation and Unclear Rules
59.1 Request for Clarification

A student or university employee who identifies uncertainty concerning the application of a university regulation may request clarification from the relevant authority.

59.2 No Automatic Exception

Uncertainty concerning a rule does not automatically create an exception to the rule.

Until clarification is provided, students should continue to comply with applicable requirements where reasonably possible.

59.3 Conflicting Provisions

When two published provisions appear to impose different requirements, the relevant authority should examine the provisions together.

The authority should consider the scope, effective date, issuing authority, and any explicit precedence or exception language.

59.4 No Assumed Priority

A provision should not be treated as superior solely because it is more convenient or favorable to a student.

Where precedence is not clear, the matter should be referred to the designated interpretation authority.

60. Amendments and Transitional Provisions
60.1 Policy Amendments

University regulations may be amended through the applicable approval process.

An amendment should identify the date from which the revised requirement applies.

60.2 Notice of Amendment

Material amendments should be communicated through an official university channel.

60.3 Transitional Arrangements

An amendment may include transitional provisions explaining how students already subject to an earlier rule will be treated.

60.4 Effective Date

Unless otherwise specified, an amended regulation applies from its stated effective date.

Students should consult the version of the regulation applicable to the relevant academic activity or decision.
